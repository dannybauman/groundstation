Learning → Works. The catalogs confirmed the 1972 MSS archive from describe_collection, not from memory; search_imagery found 20 summer scenes each year; compute_statistics and render_map both took landsat-c2-l1 items as they are. The one tool that could not help is compare_dates, which has no collection argument and searches Earth Search sentinel-2-l2a only (it returns the plain "Not enough scenes" error, not a reason). 2 things to know about the figure. First, the scaling compute_statistics applied is the item's own, and its unit is watt/steradian/square_meter/micrometer: this is an index on top-of-atmosphere radiance, not surface reflectance, so it is not comparable to a Landsat 8 or Sentinel-2 NDVI, and the 2 scenes come from 2 sensors (Landsat 2 and Landsat 3) with different gains. Second, the box holds forest, lakes and the cone, so the drop of 0.2734 in the median is the whole box, not the blast zone alone. Not checked: the share of the box that went from vegetated to bare (the tools give no per-pixel threshold count), sun angle and haze between 1979-09-12 and 1980-09-15, the qa_pixel band, and whether the east edge of the 1979 layer, which ends partway across the screenshot, is the scene's footprint or tiles that had not drawn.

Learning → Partly. The imagery side worked: the archive, the scene pick with its reasons, the statistics with a range_note that names the -0.2 offset, and a swipe map. The event side could not help, and that is a finding: active_events asks EONET for open events only (its query carries status open) and reads GDACS's current-events feed, so a closed 2015 storm is out of reach at any day count. On the figure, a fall of 0.0486 in the median is small, and the after scene is cloudier (18.53% against 11.6% scene-wide), and cloud lowers the index, so how much of the drop is wind damage and how much is cloud is not separable from these numbers (my reading). The 11.6% before is the cloud for the whole scene, not the box; the screenshot shows cumulus over the land on both sides. The land box still crosses some coast, which is why its max is 19.46 and 30.08. Not checked: a cloud mask from qa_pixel, the Landsat 7 2015-11-22 pick (4.0% cloud, but Landsat 7 has had striped gaps since 2003), and the damage track itself against a published survey.

map: Great Aletsch NDSI swipe, 2017-08-23 against 2026-08-30 ↗
full read
Our read
The tools cannot test the 5% claim, and the snow and ice index over the Great Aletsch glacier went up, not down: mean NDSI 0.2108 on 2017-08-23 against 0.2275 on 2026-08-30, +0.0167 (+7.9%). Ice volume is measured on the ground (GLAMOS, the Swiss glacier monitoring network, works from stakes, surveys and elevation models), not by a satellite index, so nothing here confirms or refutes "more than 5% of their ice volume in 2026". What the tools can show is snow and ice extent on 1 day each year. From mid August to the end of September under 20% cloud there was 1 scene in 2017 (4.10% cloud) and 10 in 2026; compare_dates picked 2026-08-30 (9.79% cloud) from the same tile, 32TMS, covering 100.0% of the box. The medians are close, 0.1048 and 0.1080. The swipe map shows the glacier tongue and its dark moraine stripes on both dates, with pale cloud over the upper left of the 2017 side (my reading of the image).

Learning → Partly. The tools did their part: 2 matched Sentinel-2 scenes 9 years apart, an NDSI on correctly scaled bands, per-scene statistics, and a swipe map. They could not do what the sentence asks, because volume is not in any of these catalogs, and the card says so rather than letting a rise of 7.9% stand in for an answer. Why the index rose is not in the output; snow on the day, cloud (9.79% in 2026, and NDSI over cloud is not snow), and the box holding rock and forest as well as ice all move a mean like this (my reading). One mean over a 0.15 by 0.15 degree box is also not glacier area: the tools give no count of pixels above a snow threshold, which is the usual way to turn NDSI into extent. A small quirk: on Earth Search the statistics key comes back as b1, not the expression. Not checked: the Rhône glacier, other late-summer dates in 2026 (10 scenes were there), a snow threshold area, and any GLAMOS figure.
