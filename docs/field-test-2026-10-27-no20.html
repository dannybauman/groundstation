Our read
Imagery can show the glacier's surface on a late-summer day, not its ice volume: over the Great Aletsch glacier the mean snow and ice index (NDSI) rose from 0.2108 on 2017-08-23 to 0.2275 on 2026-08-30, +0.0167 (+7.9%), and that is snow cover on the day, not ice volume. The 5% figure is GLAMOS's ground measurement (the Swiss glacier monitoring network, working from stakes, surveys and elevation models), which these tools cannot test, so nothing here speaks for or against "more than 5% of their ice volume in 2026". What the tools can show is the snow and ice at the surface on 1 day each year. From mid August to the end of September under 20% cloud there was 1 scene in 2017 (4.10% cloud) and 10 in 2026; compare_dates picked 2026-08-30 (9.79% cloud) from the same tile, 32TMS, covering 100.0% of the box. The medians are close, 0.1048 and 0.1080. The swipe map shows ice on both sides of the divider, the tongue with its moraine stripes on the 2026 side, and pale patches that look like cloud over the upper left of the 2017 side (my reading of the image).
